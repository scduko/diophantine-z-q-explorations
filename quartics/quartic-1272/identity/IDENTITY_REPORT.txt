Identity and fixed-constant audit of the Brox1315 construction
2026-11-06

Result

The public text determines several exact local formulas and a class of valid
quadratic compilations. It does not determine the author's complete labeled
1272-row residual list or either 1315-variable coefficient table. A new valid
compilation can establish a new polynomial and its statistics. It cannot, by
matching the prose or aggregate counts alone, establish identity with the
unavailable Brox1315 artifact.

A fresh read-only GitHub API query found main still at
f4d1b5e6c23d4adeb3ae1f986ab0bf87884bdff6. The recursive tree was untruncated and
contained only the README, benchmark CSV and JSON, archive-import instructions,
and paper. There were zero releases. The public archive-import instructions
explicitly say the research and certificate directories have not been imported.
No new public artifact was recovered.

Specified choices

* The coefficient field is Q(sqrt(2)), represented by two rational components.
  Rational affine arithmetic costs no new coordinate. A product or arbitrary
  quadratic expression can be assigned to one new rational coordinate. Field
  products, inverses, and guarded quotients use the two displayed component
  equations. These are exact rules at lines 4367-4405.
* The quartic retains the scalar preparation coordinate. It uses comparison
  precision 52, beta precision 16, scalar order 32, and kappa=3^(2^28).
  Lines 4138-4147 and 4567-4573 fix these choices.
* The original semilocal anchor constant is C=3^(2^26), and the displayed
  precompiled anchor is u^2+6v^2-2w^2-3(f0^2+f0*f1-f1^2)^2+2*C^4.
  See lines 4170-4175. The fixed height scales are x0^2 and (1+x1^2)^2,
  at lines 3352-3364. The weight powers are explicit at lines 4272-4279.
* The direct Lucas test is F(U,V,W)=V^2-U*(U-2)*V*W+(U-2)^2*W^2-1.
  The text fixes the intermediary expressions A=U*(U-2), B=V*W,
  C=(U-2)*W, and F=V^2-A*B+C^2-1 at lines 4575-4599.
* The polynomial block sizes are b=3 for G12 and b=4 for G3, with powers
  and Horner evaluation as described at lines 4407-4421. Sharing the
  symmetric-product coefficient lists across detectors is explicit at
  lines 4423-4437 and 4598-4602.
* Eight Boolean coordinates encode sign and seven listed prime factors.
  Their defining equations and correction product are at lines 4458-4468.
* The final direct anchor is R=-3*A^2+16*u^2+96*v^2-32*w^2+32*kappa,
  where A=X52+X53-X54 and (u,v,w)=(X55,X56,X57). The text claims the
  three private coordinates occur nowhere else, at lines 4680-4688.
* The final affine translation, its inverse, determinant 1/64, and translated
  anchor are completely explicit at lines 4690-4708. These identities pass
  the new exact check. They require no unavailable artifact locally.
* Input index 0 and sparse encoding [coordinate multiset, constant power,
  integer coefficient] are explicit at lines 4543-4551 and 4878-4881.

Choices not specified sufficiently to reproduce identity

* The complete ordered source-equation list and its exact compiler input are
  absent. The text describes how to derive a mathematical system, but does
  not print the compiler's source syntax tree or every lowered residual.
* The ordering and names of all 1315 coordinates are absent. A few indices
  are fixed by the anchor and the Gaussian restriction, the latter at
  lines 7920-7942. These do not determine the remaining indices.
* Common expressions are to be reused, but no canonicalization, rewrite
  order, expression hashing, multiplication association, or complete cache
  trace is given. Polynomial block sizes alone do not fix gate identity.
* Lines 4473-4486 permit rational row addition and quadratic-preserving
  elimination. They do not list the 11 row-index pairs and multipliers, or
  the 60 defining rows, removed coordinates, substitutions, and remappings.
  The counts at lines 4626-4628 cannot recover these missing operations.
* The mathematical row normalization rule is clear: clear rational
  denominators and divide the specialized integer coefficient gcd, at
  lines 4488-4490. The actual factors are absent. A row sign is immaterial
  to the sum of its square, but still matters to byte identity of residual
  lists. A formal coefficient gcd in Z[S] need not be the specialized gcd.
* The final full coefficient table, full incidence table, and verification
  receipts are absent. The stated facts that only three coordinates occur
  in the anchor and that the rest of the coefficients meet a bound remain
  full-table assertions, not consequences of the local anchor identity.

The loss of identity is mathematical, not just a compression-format issue.
For example, both {x,y} and {x+y,y} have the same common zero set and are
connected by an allowed row addition, but their sums of squares differ.
Variable reordering and different gate sharing can likewise change a labeled
table without changing its existential projection. A reconstructed artifact
therefore needs its own hash and a new statement of what has been verified.

Why a symbolic fixed-S table is an exact integer polynomial

Let S0=3^(2^27). A finite table of triples (m,k,c), with m a finite
multiset of coordinate indices, k a nonnegative integer, and c an integer,
defines the element

  P(X)=sum_(m,k,c) c*S0^k*product_(i in m) Xi

of Z[X0,...,Xn]. Finiteness and integrality follow directly. S0 is an
explicit fixed integer, so it contributes neither a quantified coordinate
nor polynomial degree. No decimal expansion is needed to define P exactly.
Replace each kappa^j by S^(2j) to compare the two coefficient encodings.

Evaluation S -> S0 is a ring homomorphism. Thus exact convolution and
substitution identities in Q[S][X] prove the corresponding integer or
rational polynomial identities after specialization. Equality in Q[S][X]
is sufficient for equality at S0. It is not necessary in general because
specialization has a kernel. To conclude that a nonzero formal coefficient
survives specialization, use a separate nonvanishing certificate.

For p(S)=a_d*S^d+...+a_0 with a_d nonzero, S0>=1 gives

  |sum_(j<d) a_j*S0^j| <= S0^(d-1)*sum_(j<d)|a_j|.

If |a_d|*S0 > sum_(j<d)|a_j|, p(S0) has the sign of a_d. Since
S0 > 2^(2^27), one can prove this using only the bit length of the tiny
rational ratio sum|a_j|/|a_d|. check_identity.py deliberately uses this
sufficient test and rejects coefficients for which it cannot certify the
inequality. No 134-million-bit power of two is constructed either.
Multiplying by a positive power of S preserves signs for Laurent polynomials.

Support is then computed by grouping entries by coordinate monomial and
certifying nonzero specialization of every remaining coefficient. Counting
formal triples alone is wrong when a monomial has more than one S power.
The degree is the largest coordinate-multiset length among surviving
coefficients. An exact coefficient comparison applies the sign test to
candidate(S)-p(S) and candidate(S)+p(S), proving candidate(S)>=|p(S)|.
Equality at one coefficient proves the maximum is attained.

For rational coefficient polynomials with nonnegative exponents, choose a
positive common denominator D. Integrality is equivalent to divisibility
of the integer numerator polynomial N(S0) by D. Compute S0 modulo D as
pow(3,2^27,D), then N(S0) modulo D. This uses an exponent with 28 bits,
not an expanded S0. Negative powers require extra divisibility evidence
and should be rejected by a verifier that only implements this criterion.

Primitive content needs its own proof after specialization. The two
polynomials S+1 and S+3 have no common formal polynomial factor, but both
are even at S0. A literal coefficient 1 proves integer content 1 immediately.
More generally, a gcd of 1 among S-free integer coefficients suffices.
If a nonzero S-free coefficient bounds the possible gcd, modular checks
against its prime divisors can finish the proof. One must not silently
replace any of these tests by formal content 1 in Z[S].

Local anchor results, independently checked

check_identity.py verifies the two affine-map compositions, determinant,
anchor substitution, and square substitution exactly over Q[S]. It writes
all local rows and expanded local coefficients in anchor_certificate.json.
The original and translated anchor squares have respectively 49 and 79
coordinate monomials. Their private-coordinate supports have sizes 27 and
64. In the translated square there is only one S^2 coefficient, 64*S^2
at X55^2; the largest coefficient multiplier of S has absolute value 288,
and the largest S-free local coefficient has absolute value 540. No local
coordinate monomial receives more than one S power.

These local facts do not prove a global support count. Shared-coordinate
terms can overlap with other residual squares. The source reports the
necessary overlaps, but the full table must verify them. Similarly the
global height 64*S^2 follows only after checking the rest of the coefficients
are smaller and the final polynomial is primitive. The source's bound
2^1020 for the rest, plus input coefficient 1, would suffice. The local
checker verifies 64*S0^2>288*S0 and 64*S0^2>2^1020 symbolically, as well as
64*S0^2<2^429496736 using 3^5<2^8.

Concrete checks for a complete reconstructed certificate

1. Pin the precise source bytes, reconstruction source code, schema, and
   ordered source rows. Assign fresh immutable hashes. State that the
   result is independently reconstructed unless original hashes match.
2. Validate every coordinate reference, introduced coordinate, rational
   coefficient, and fixed-constant power. Keep input index 0 immutable.
3. Match every structural source equation to its explicit lowered row.
   Replay gates from their earlier inputs. Check the nonzero denominator
   premises or retained inverse equations separately from polynomial
   identities. A correct algebraic trace alone cannot prove those premises.
4. Replay each row addition with distinct retained source row, then every
   elimination with a nonzero constant coefficient for the removed
   coordinate. Check the defining remainder is independent of that
   coordinate, substitute in every row, and verify degree at most two.
5. Check each primitive normalization by a nonzero rational scaling
   identity, exact specialized integrality, and specialized gcd 1.
6. Reconstruct all final residuals and independently convolve their squares.
   Compare every coefficient and every active-coordinate incidence. Do
   not accept a count or a table hash as a substitute for this comparison.
7. Verify the three private anchor coordinates occur only in the anchor
   before applying the displayed translation. Check both affine-map
   compositions and the full literal-polynomial identity.
8. Collect equal coordinate monomials, certify coefficient nonvanishing,
   degree, total support, active variable count, primitive content, and
   maximum coefficient at S0 with the methods above.
9. Record separately the compiler/certificate result, agreement with the
   manuscript's reported statistics, identity with an original artifact,
   and the external arithmetic hypotheses. These are different claims.

Run the local audit with

  python identity/check_identity.py

The result is checks.json. Its PASS is scoped to the local identities and
fixed-S proof criteria, and explicitly does not verify the full Brox1315.

Independent check of the newly reconstructed tables

The team subsequently produced a new 1272-coordinate compilation. The
separate script check_reconstructed_tables.py imports only the polynomial
arithmetic in this auditor's check_identity.py. It does not import the
producer or compiler. It checks both tables directly and writes
reconstructed_table_checks.json.

The direct table with SHA-256
5acd7ebf8c09c7d95f66c16f6c5594fdbbde062a0f3204068e8f88b7fd4b54ba
has 47986 encoded entries, 47984 coordinate monomials, 1272 active
coordinates, degree 4, and primitive integer content 1. Its maximum
coefficient is the constant coefficient, recorded exactly in the JSON.

The translated table with SHA-256
02e4771ba6e6c2abc749e33e8440cc256939f48005d5102fba7d9853489c8586
has 48001 encoded entries and coordinate monomials, 1272 active
coordinates, degree 4, primitive integer content 1, and exact maximum
coefficient 64*S^2 at X79^2. Both input fourth-power coefficients are 1.
The checker proves every specialized coefficient is nonzero and compares
every coefficient with the computed maximum. The largest sufficient
domination bound needs 5337 bits, far below the available 134217728 bits.
The reconstructed tables need not obey the manuscript's 2^1020 bound for
all other coefficients; the direct dominance checks establish the maximum
without assuming that bound.

The new raw anchor starts with a different scaling from the author's final
anchor. Direct inspection and exact substitution show

  q_new = 16 * (q_old composed with T),

where T is the displayed affine map and q_old is the new builder's raw
anchor. The checker verifies both inverse compositions, that the private
coordinates occur in no other row, and that all 1260 other rows are equal.
Consequently the residual zero sets are affinely bijective over every
characteristic-zero field, and their sums of squares have equal rational
input projection. The literal raw sums of squares instead satisfy

  P_new - (P_old composed with T) = 255*(q_old composed with T)^2.

This nonzero difference must not be reported as a literal polynomial
substitution identity. It does not obstruct rational zero-set equivalence.
Later independent compiler-certificate replay is responsible for connecting
the raw rows to these reduced tables. A separate source audit is responsible
for connecting the builder's arithmetic equations to the conditional
mathematical definition. Neither follows from the coefficient check alone.

These hashes and statistics are those of the newly reconstructed objects.
They differ from the reported Brox1315 coordinate and monomial counts and
do not recover the missing original artifact.
