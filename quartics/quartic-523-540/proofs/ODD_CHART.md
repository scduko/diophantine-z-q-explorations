# An exact odd-index chart in four extra rational coordinates

Let K=Q(sqrt(2)), epsilon=1+sqrt(2), d=3-sqrt(2), D=d^2, and
E: y^2=x^3-Dx. Retain the source's established group statement
2E(K)=Z P, and put R0=(epsilon,2sqrt(2)). Direct substitution and
the duplication formula give R0 in E(K) and 2R0=P, with exactly
the source's sign of P.

For a square-abscissa chart point use

    x=h^2, j^2=x^2-D, Q=(x,hj).

Add t,r in K and the two field equations

    t^2 = 2(x+j),
    th+x+j = epsilon*r^2.                         (1)

These are four quadratic rational residuals in four additional rational
coordinates. No multiplication gate or inverse witness is required.
The projected chart points are exactly P+4E(K), equivalently the odd
multiples of P. The added constraints may select one of the two sign
representations (h,j) of a point; the assertion concerns the projected
point Q, and every odd Q admits a representation. There are no omitted odd multiples and no extra finite
exceptional branches.

## The squareclass homomorphism

Write [c] for c in K*/K*2. The usual two-isogeny descent homomorphism is

    delta(O)=1, delta((0,0))=[-D]=[-1],
    delta((a,b))=[a] for a != 0.

For completeness its multiplicativity on the dense nonexceptional addition
chart follows from

    x(R+S)*x(R)*x(S)
       = ((x(R)*y(S)-x(S)*y(R))/(x(R)-x(S)))^2.

The exceptional cases can be checked explicitly. If R=S and y(R)!=0,
then x(2R)=((x(R)^2+D)/(2y(R)))^2, as required. If R=-S,
the two squareclasses have product one, including the 2-torsion cases.
Addition by T0=(0,0) sends a nonzero abscissa a to -D/a, so contributes
the factor [-D]. If R+S=T0, the same formula gives x(R)x(S)=-D.
Identity summands are immediate. All other pairs with equal abscissas
are already doubling or inverse pairs; all pairs with different nonzero
abscissas and sum different from T0 are covered by the displayed identity.
This proves multiplicativity on every K-rational pair.

Every R in E(K) has a unique expression

    R=n R0+T,  n in Z, T in E[2](K),

because 2R=nP and the kernel of doubling is E[2](K). The full rational
2-torsion is generated by T0=(0,0) and Td=(d,0). Therefore

    delta(nR0+aT0+bTd)=[epsilon]^n[-1]^a[d]^b.

The classes [epsilon],[-1],[d] are independent. If
(-1)^a d^b epsilon^c is a square, with a,b,c in {0,1}, its norm
7^b(-1)^c is a rational square. Its sign forces c=0 and its
7-adic valuation forces b=0. Finally -1 is not a square in the real
field K, so a=0. Consequently

    delta(R)=[epsilon]  iff  R in R0+2E(K).        (2)

This argument uses only the inherited statement 2E(K)=ZP, its displayed
halfpoint, and elementary squareclasses; it introduces no new rank or
generator assertion.

## From the augmented chart to an odd multiple

Set u=t^2=2(x+j) and a=th+u/2, b=at. The chart implies

    u(x-u/4)=D,
    a^2-u*a=D,
    b^2=a^3-Da.

The first identity forces t!=0. If a=0, the second forces D=0,
which is impossible. Thus b!=0. The tangent slope at (a,b) is
m=h+t: the identity 2bm=3a^2-D follows by expansion. Duplication gives

    m^2-2a=h^2=x,
    m(a-x)-b=h(u/2-x)=hj.

Hence Q=2(a,b). Equation (1) says a=epsilon*r^2, so r!=0
and delta((a,b))=[epsilon]. By (2), (a,b) belongs to R0+2E(K),
and Q belongs to P+4E(K).

## From an odd multiple to the augmented chart

Let Q=(2n+1)P. Choose R=(2n+1)R0, so 2R=Q and
R belongs to R0+2E(K). Write R=(a,b). R is non-torsion, so
a,b are nonzero. Define

    t=b/a, h=(a^2+D)/(2b), x=h^2, j=t^2/2-x.

The duplication identities above give Q=(x,hj) and j^2=x^2-D.
By (2), a/epsilon is a square in K; choose r with a=epsilon*r^2.
Then both equations (1) hold. All quantities are K-rational.

For every chart solution h and j are nonzero by the existing chart
lemma (or its elementary real-embedding and norm arguments). Thus the
unchanged parameter denominators remain valid. The new constraints add
no divisions or denominator guards.

## Implementation

`patch.py` exports `patch(source_string)` for the current h,j builder.
It adds these witnesses and equations for A,S,T,Z only. H and the four
U_i retain the original square-abscissa chart. The total increase is
16 rational coordinates and 16 quadratic residuals, with zero new
arithmetic gates. The input coordinate is untouched.

This is an exact characterization of the odd coset. Replacing unrestricted
root points by this coset changes the source's witness space; completeness
of the larger integer-definition construction must separately construct
odd root indices. The chart lemma alone does not supply that arithmetic
completeness argument.
