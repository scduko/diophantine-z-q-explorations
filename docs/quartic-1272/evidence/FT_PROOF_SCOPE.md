# Scope of the Frei–Tinková proof check

The Version 5 appendix uses Theorem 1.4 of Frei–Tinková, arXiv:2408.17441v3, as an external theorem. Its application was checked. Its entire proof has not been independently established in this audit. This distinction remains after the local repair below. The accompanying `FT_PROOF_DEPENDENCIES.json` records the inspected chain and the remaining work; it is not a certificate that every node is proved.

The author page lists the work as **Mathematika, to appear**, as checked on 6 November 2026. We found no final journal text or journal DOI. The cached author page and Crossref title search are included in `sources/`. Absence of a final published text is not evidence that the theorem is false. Theorem 1.4 is the unconditional fixed Artin-symbol theorem. The conditional primitive-root theorem is Theorem 1.2, and its Hooley/HRH argument is not an input to the application here.

## A corrected inverse-theorem application

There is an actual mismatch in the proof of Lemma 9.3, `FT-v3.tex:2924–2948`, which Section 10 reuses for the Artin-symbol theorem. It requests the majorant with complexity `D=4^u`, derives small `U^{2u}` norm for `nu−1`, and then applies Dodos–Kanellopoulos Theorem 5.1 to a large `U^{u+1}[N]` norm. The cited theorem requires a small `U^{2s}` majorant norm for target `U^s`, so here it requires `U^{2u+2}`. The displayed weaker norm does not supply that hypothesis.

The repair is to choose `D=4^{u+1}` in Lemma 9.1. The `2u+2` cube contains `2^{2u+2}=D` pairwise nonproportional forms, has `2u+3≤D` variables for `u≥1`, and has coefficients zero or one. Lemma 9.1 therefore estimates every subproduct in the centered expansion. Inclusion–exclusion gives `||nu−1||_{U^{2u+2}}=o(1)`. All constants remain fixed before the size parameter tends to infinity. The positive rescaling and truncation already in Lemma 9.3 supply the domination hypothesis. The theorem yields the same bounded `u`-step nilsequence. No arithmetic hypothesis, final variable count or claimed prime-pattern conclusion changes.

The exact Dodos–Kanellopoulos statement was checked in arXiv:1610.00487v4, pp. 12–13, `DK-v4.txt:706–726`. Its published work is *Acta Arithmetica* 203 (2022), 251–270, DOI 10.4064/aa210728-27-2. The publisher page confirms online publication on 10 May 2022. The checked arXiv version is dated 9 June 2022. We did not compare the final typeset statement. The independent reviewer checked both the mismatch and the correction. The manuscript contains the correction as `rem:v5-ft-norm-repair`, with the full theorem hypotheses and this source qualification.

## What the proof route actually requires

Theorem 1.4 follows in Section 10 from the Artin-weight Gowers estimate, Proposition 2.1, and the transference argument of Section 9. The Gowers estimate uses the ordinary prime majorant, the repaired relative inverse theorem application, and Theorem 2.3. Theorem 2.3 compares the Artin weight with its ordinary-prime density model against arbitrary bounded-complexity nilsequences. Its proof at `FT-v3.tex:2550–2575` uses factorization, progression estimates and mean-zero equidistributed estimates from Section 6.

The main new arithmetic input is Theorem 2.4, not a direct consequence of the published ordinary Möbius theorem. It bounds a finite-order Hecke-character twist of the ideal von Mangoldt function, evaluated on ideal norms, against a mean-zero equidistributed nilsequence. Its field, character modulus and nilmanifold complexity are fixed. Section 5 introduces norm-polynomial estimates, an ideal convolution/Vaughan identity, an ideal Type I/II alternative and the dyadic estimate in Lemma 5.8.

We inspected the norm-coefficient divisibility argument, the use of unit fundamental domains and polynomial subsequences in Proposition 5.2, the divisor comparisons, the convolution identity, the setup of both Type I and Type II cases, and the passage from ideal sums to Chebotarev weights in Lemma 6.2. The last passage uses character orthogonality over the cyclic subgroup generated by a representative of the conjugacy class and discards higher-degree prime ideals and prime powers. These are substantive proof steps, not replacements by a density assertion.

The quantitative Leibman theorem and factorization theorem were checked in the published Green–Tao paper. They provide horizontal-character obstructions and the decomposition into smooth, equidistributed and rational parts. Their statements alone do not prove FT's ideal-norm estimates. Lemma 5.8 explicitly adapts the published Type I/II argument and gives only a sketch of the Type II adaptation before referring to the published final combinatorial step. We have not completed an independent line-by-line proof of this entire adaptation, the dyadic passage to Theorem 2.4, all metric/rationality bounds in factorization, or every ancillary published input. No claim of such closure is made.

## Why the checked published alternatives do not close the gap in scope

Kane's one-linear-relation result does not supply arbitrary finite-complexity cube correlations. Those correlations are needed by the Gowers-norm transfer used in the appendix. The ordinary polynomial-primes theorem and dense-subset recurrence do not by themselves provide the explicit lower bound in the ray cutoff that is compared with the large-prime tail. The general transference theorem of Bienvenu–Shao–Teräväinen still needs an appropriate nilsequence estimate for the Artin weights. Naming it does not remove FT's arithmetic work.

Accordingly, Version 5 supplies a reviewed derivation of the needed prime-pattern theorem relative to FT Theorem 1.4, documents the local correction in its proof route, and preserves the accepted-preprint qualification. A proof using only independently checked published external statements remains unestablished in this audit. This is a limit of the completed verification, not a counterexample to FT Theorem 1.4 or to the simultaneous prime statement.
